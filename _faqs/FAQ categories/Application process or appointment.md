<p><strong>Will there be a transitional phase where a psychiatrist’s expert evidence is still admissible even though he or she is not a member of the Panel?</strong>
</p>
<p>Yes. To assist psychiatrists to transition to the new regime, there will
be a transitional phase where expert evidence by psychiatrists who are
not on the Panel will remain admissible in criminal proceedings if any
of the following scenarios apply:</p>
<table style="minWidth: 75px">
<colgroup>
<col>
<col>
<col>
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr>
<th rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Transitional Scenario 1: Where the expert opinion is adduced in Court
between 1 January 2026 and 28 February 2026 (TBC)</p>
</th>
<th rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Transitional Scenario 2: Where the psychiatrist has submitted his application
between 1 January 2026 and 28 February 2026 (TBC); and</p>
<ul data-tight="true" class="tight">
<li>
<p>The application is pending; or</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>There is a pending appeal against the Selection Committee’s decision or
the psychiatrist is still within the timelines for filing an appeal.</p>
</li>
</ul>
</th>
<th rowspan="1" colspan="1">
<p>Transitional Scenario 3: Where the psychiatrist's treatment or assessment
of the subject had started before 1 January 2026 (TBC).</p>
</th>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If none of the scenarios above apply, the psychiatrist’s expert opinion
will not be admissible in criminal proceedings.</p>
